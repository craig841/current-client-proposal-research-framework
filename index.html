Devil's Advocate Pass — Strategy Gap-Check

Slot: Step 5.5 in the client strategy framework — after "Second-round synthesis," before the draft moves to production for the client deck.

Job: find the weakest part of the strategy before a client or competitor does. This is not a rewrite and not a vibe check. It's a fixed set of adversarial questions the draft has to survive, run the same way every time so it can't be talked out of finding something.

When to run it

  • Every time, after the second-round draft is produced — not optional, not "if there's time"
  • Mandatory when the draft "requires little editing" — that's precisely the moment scrutiny tends to drop
  • Especially important when: the fee or scope is increasing, you're recommending a channel change from the status quo, or the client is likely to have a skeptical stakeholder in the room

The checklist — fixed, don't skip an item even if the answer feels obvious

  1. Weakest assumption — what does this strategy depend on being true that hasn't actually been confirmed?
  2. Unproven claim — what's the most confident-sounding statement in the draft that isn't backed by a number, a source, or client-confirmed data?
  3. Competitor attack — if a rival agency saw this pitch, what's the first thing they'd pick apart?
  4. Missing client data — what would change this recommendation if we had client CRM, analytics, or sales data we don't currently have?
  5. Toughest stakeholder — if the most skeptical person in the client's room pushed back hard, which part collapses first?
  6. Comfort vs rigour — is this the most correct recommendation, or the most comfortable one to present? Where did the process choose ease over pressure-testing?
  7. What got dropped — what did an earlier round surface that quietly disappeared from this draft, and why?

Output format

Not prose — a short table, so a Red can't hide in a paragraph:

#QuestionFlagWhyWhat resolves it
1 Weakest assumption — —

Red — must be resolved or explicitly accepted as a known risk before production Amber — needs a one-line owner note Green — needs nothing further

The prompt — paste into Claude / Cowork against the draft + original client brief

Act as Devil's Advocate. You are reviewing [strategy draft v2] against [the original
client brief]. Your only job is to find where this strategy is weakest — not to
improve it, not to rewrite it, not to soften the finding.

clearly fine — every item needs an explicit answer, not a pass.

If you genuinely can't find a weakness for an item, say so plainly rather than
inventing one. A false positive wastes this pass as much as a missed one does.

A note on calibration

If this pass comes back clean — no reds, no ambers — across several client strategies in a row, treat that as a signal to check, not a compliment to accept. Either the upstream steps (research, Cowork review, NotebookLM insight) are genuinely tight, or the checklist itself has gone soft. Revisit the seven questions every quarter and sharpen anything that's stopped finding real issues.
